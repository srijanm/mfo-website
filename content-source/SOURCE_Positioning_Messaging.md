## **Who we're for**

1. Foreign Income Freelancers (Software Developers, Digital Marketing, Consultants, Tutors)  
2. Domestic Freelancers  
3. Content Creators  
4. Other Service Businesses like Doctors, Landlords, Lawyers, etc

Traits

1. **People in their first few years of earning without a salary.**  
2. **Usually the first person in their family without a payslip.**  
3. Many of them don't call themselves self-employed. Someone on a US contract earning $60k has a manager, standups, and a laptop the company paid for.   
4. He has no Form 16, no TDS, no PF, and no employer in India. On paper he runs a business. In his head he has a job.  
5. Maximum 5 clients per month (CAs typically ask how many transactions do you have, these people have 1 to 5 client invoices monthly and recurring)

The audience isn't "people who work for themselves." Half of them won't put their hand up. It's **people whose money doesn't arrive as a salary**.

We go after the moment they start rather than the job they do, for two reasons.

* People cling to what they already do, so "switch to us" is a harder sell than "start with us." The one window where habits are still loose is a change in circumstances. Becoming self-employed is that window, and nobody is standing in it.

What that mismatch feels like from the client's side, from Reddit:

> "My dad and my grandfather have all been salarymen, no business acumen, no insight into all this."

### **Nothing goes wrong in year one**

**Income tax and gst notices come 5-8 years later. Banks freeze bank accounts of foreign income earners after 2-3 years of waiting**

Every disaster in the research is backdated. The problem shows up in month thirty and belongs to month four.

* GST registration they should have done eighteen months ago, found when a client refuses to pay without a GST invoice.  
* Advance tax interest that arrives as a surprise every single year.  
* TDS a client deducted but never deposited, so it never reaches 26AS and can't be claimed.  
* An LUT nobody mentioned, so two years of export invoices sit exposed.  
* A free product from a brand that turns out to have been taxable income.

We sell the absence of that. It's also the hardest thing to sell, because someone who feels fine today doesn't buy anything.

## **Who they'd use instead**

Before they go looking, most first-timers do nothing for a year or two. Delay is the real enemy at that stage, and there's nothing to position against, because they aren't comparing anything.

Once they go looking, they compare us to a CA, usually a family or hometown one. That comparison happens in their head at the moment of decision, so that's the one we position against.

| Alternative | Where it breaks |
| ----- | ----- |
| **A family or hometown CA** | Good at what he's done for thirty years. Has usually never filed for someone paid by a platform, a creator taking barter deals, or a contractor invoicing in dollars. Files once a year and warns you about nothing. Often won't take a first-timer. |
| **ClearTax or Quicko** | Cheap and competent at the filing. Tells you how to file, never whether you should have registered for GST last March. |
| **Vakilsearch, IndiaFilings, RegisterKaro** | The headline price isn't the price. Work splits across handoffs so nobody owns your outcome. One customer paid ₹5,000 for an LUT, then filed it himself in twenty minutes. |

None of them warn you about anything. They wait for you to ask, and by the time you know what to ask, you're late.

## **Value proposition**

### **The problem has three layers**

* **On the surface.** I don't know whether I need GST, a company, or advance tax. I don't know whether what I've already done is wrong.  
* **Underneath.** I feel stupid and exposed. Something might be piling up against me and I'd have no idea. There's nobody to ask.

Everyone in this market speaks to the first layer. The second is where the money sits. The third is where loyalty comes from.

### **The promise**

**MyFinanceOfficer sets up and runs the compliance side, so nothing is ever quietly going wrong in the background.**

### **What we do at each stage**

| Stage | What's happening | What we do |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **First income** | Do I register anything? Can this money land in my savings account? | Tell them what they need and don't need yet. Often the answer is "nothing right now, and here's when that changes." |
| **First year** | Invoicing, what's claimable, first ITR, advance tax nobody mentioned | Set it up properly, file on time, keep the four advance tax dates on our calendar rather than theirs. |
| **₹20 lakh** | GST becomes compulsory, usually discovered late | Register on time, file the LUT where exports are involved, keep the paperwork trail intact. |
| **₹50 lakh** | Audit threshold, and they quietly become a TDS deductor | Flag it a year early rather than a month late. |
| **₹75 lakh and past** | Presumptive ceiling, entity structure, real planning | Advise honestly, including when the answer is "don't incorporate yet." |

Publishing this ladder is a differentiator by itself, since "what do I do next" is the question nobody answers at scale. Seeing the whole arc is also what lets us warn someone about stage four while they're in stage two.

### **Proof, with no clients yet**

Our trust will come from external validations like lots of reviews, recommendations, the video of me talking sense and not sounding like a full time influencer.

Everyone else in this market counts things. *5000+ Expert Tax CAs. 8M+ Users. 612,845+ successful engagements.* We can't, so trust has to come from how we work.

Proof only lands when the reader already understands the problem it solves. Telling a first-timer "you keep your own GST portal credentials" means nothing to someone who doesn't know he needs a GST number. So it's staged.

**Someone who's just started earning:**

**Someone deciding whether to hire a CA:**

## **Messaging**

### **Headline**

> **Half the work you do didn't exist when your family's CA started out.**

> MyFinanceOfficer is a modern CA firm for modern professions. Setup, registration, GST and filings for people who don't get a payslip.

The headline states a fact rather than a claim, which is why it survives scrutiny. It also does the job of naming the mismatch without attacking anyone's family CA, which is a line we can't afford to cross. The subhead carries the description, so a reader knows what we sell before they scroll.

One judgement call worth flagging. "Your family's CA" assumes they have one, and part of our own thesis is that many of them don't. It still works, because the archetype is familiar to every Indian reader whether or not they've used one. If it grates, the neutral version is "when most CA firms started out," which is truer and duller.

### **Descriptor**

**A modern CA firm for modern professions.**

For the tab title, the footer, the intro line, anywhere someone needs to know what we are in six words. "Modern" is a word anyone can claim, so it only holds up if the proof underneath it is specific. Every page it appears on needs to name actual work: platform income, barter deals, foreign contracts, multiple clients.

Alternative when we need to name the customer rather than ourselves: **A CA firm for people who don't get a payslip.**

### **The line that works on its own**

**You do the work. We do everything the tax department expects from you.**

For ads, social, and anywhere the line has to carry the whole offer without a page around it.

### **The line for people who think they're fine**

**Nothing goes wrong in your first year.**

*It goes wrong in your third, about something from your first.*

Most of this audience doesn't know they have a problem. This is the line that puts one in front of them, and it's the right opener for cold traffic and content.

### **Claim hierarchy**

**The one thing:** with MyFinanceOfficer you always know where you stand, and nothing is quietly going wrong.

1. **We start you off right.** Registrations, structure, GST only if you need it, invoicing that holds up.  
2. **We run it, not you.** Every deadline is ours. You hear from us before you'd have remembered.  
3. **We're on the hook.** Fixed annual fee quoted upfront, a named signatory, and we answer the notice.

Everything else is evidence for one of those three.

### **Objections**

* **"I don't earn enough for this yet."** Often true. Say so. The version of us that tells someone to come back later is the one they come back to.  
* **"My family has a CA."** Never attack him, because attacking the family CA attacks the family. Make it about fit. He's good at what he's done for thirty years and this isn't it.  
* **"Can't I just use ClearTax?"** For the filing, sometimes yes. The gap is the twelve months before it.  
* **"Is this cheaper than a normal CA?"** Probably not. It's fixed, and you know it before you start. The grievance in this market is surprise, not price.  
* **"Are you just using AI?"** We use software so a small team can do the work of a big one. A person signs your return.

### **Words**

**Use:** file, in writing, on time, signed, fixed fee, before you start, we'll tell you when, properly, the actual answer, what this means for you.

**Never use.** Three or more Indian competitors use each of these, so saying them tells the reader nothing:

> expert CAs, X+ certified professionals, transparent pricing, no hidden charges, 100% online, hassle-free, one-stop shop, all-in-one, end-to-end, India's \#1 / largest / most trusted / first, AI-powered, file in minutes, focus on your growth we'll handle the rest, maximum refund, starting at ₹999

Skip "boring," "tedious" and "paperwork." They make light of something the reader finds frightening. Don't call the rules "simple" or "easy" either. They aren't, and saying so makes the reader feel stupid, which is the wound we're treating.

### **Voice**

Plain and specific. Short sentences. Numbers wherever we have them. A competent person explaining something clearly.

Empathy before authority. They're embarrassed before they're confused, and leading with expertise reads as condescension.

One hard rule: **we never sell with fear.** Frightening people into buying is what the worst operators in this market do. One CA used notice-fear to extract over ₹2 lakh a year from a first-timer. Being the opposite of that is most of our differentiation. State a consequence once, flat, and move on.
